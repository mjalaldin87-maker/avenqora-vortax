Why online research needs a process

The internet contains useful research, outdated material, advertisements, opinions and inaccurate claims. A good research process helps students find relevant information without accepting the first result as automatically correct.

Start with a clear question

Turn a broad assignment or curiosity into a specific question. Instead of searching for a single broad word, add context and the information you actually need.

Improve your search terms

  • Use specific phrases.
  • Add the topic and context.
  • Use quotation marks for an exact phrase when useful.
  • Try alternative terms and synonyms.
  • Search for primary sources when appropriate.

Check the source

Look at who published the information, when it was published, what evidence is provided and whether the author has relevant expertise. A polished page is not automatically a reliable source.

Cross-check important claims

For important facts, compare multiple credible sources. If several independent sources agree and trace their claims to strong evidence, confidence can increase. When sources disagree, investigate why rather than simply choosing the statement you prefer.

Five-question source check

Who published it? When? What evidence is given? What is the purpose? Can the important claim be confirmed elsewhere?

Keep research notes

Record useful links, titles, authors, dates and the specific information you used. Good notes make assignments easier to reference and reduce the chance of losing the original source.

Use AI responsibly

AI tools can help explain concepts and suggest directions for research, but students should verify important factual claims against appropriate sources and follow their institution's academic rules.

Worked example: turn a broad topic into a research question

Broad topic: renewable energy. A more researchable question is: “What factors affect rooftop solar adoption by households in a specific region?” The focused question helps you decide which evidence, geography and date range matter.

  1. Identify key concepts and useful synonyms; try targeted queries instead of repeating the same broad search.
  2. Use official datasets, original research or institutional guidance where they fit the question, and read beyond the search-result snippet.
  3. Check publication date, method, sample or scope, author and possible conflicts of interest.
  4. Follow important claims back to their original source; confirm quotations and numbers in context.
  5. Record the URL, title, author or organization, date, relevant passage and your own summary.

Research integrity checklist

Have I represented the source accurately? Have I separated quotation from paraphrase? Have I cited the original work? Have I noted uncertainty or limitations? Have I checked whether AI-generated references actually exist?

AI tools can help brainstorm questions or summarize text you provide, but they are not proof. Verify important citations and facts against reliable original sources, and follow your institution's rules on disclosure and permitted use.

Quick review

Good online research combines precise questions, better searches, source evaluation, cross-checking and organized notes. These skills are useful for university assignments, projects, professional work and everyday decisions.
