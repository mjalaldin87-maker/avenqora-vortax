Safe Browsing: Checking Links, Downloads and Website Security

A browser is one of the main ways people access information, school resources and online services. Safe browsing combines careful link checks, trusted downloads, browser updates and good account habits.

Prepared by Zia CarterNovember 5, 2026Beginner lesson
Back to Cybersecurity lessons

What you will learn

  • Check a website address before entering sensitive information
  • Choose safer sources for software and downloads
  • Understand browser warnings and security features

Start by checking the full web address

Before entering a password or payment detail, look carefully at the website address. A fraudulent site may use a name that resembles a genuine service, with a small spelling change, extra word or different domain ending. Do not rely only on a logo or page design; both can be copied.

For important accounts, use a bookmark you created earlier or type the official address yourself. Be cautious when a message asks you to sign in through an unexpected link.

Understand HTTPS and the padlock

HTTPS encrypts the connection between a browser and a website. This helps protect information while it travels across the network. However, HTTPS does not prove that a website is honest or that the business behind it is legitimate. A fraudulent website can also use an encrypted connection.

Check the domain and the reason you are being asked to provide information. Encryption is one security signal, not a complete trust decision.

Download software from trusted sources

Prefer the official developer website, an approved application store or the software provider's documented update mechanism. Avoid installers from pop-up advertisements, file-sharing pages or messages from unknown senders. Be especially careful when a download asks you to disable a security tool or run it with administrator privileges.

For school or workplace devices, follow the organization's approved software process. An application that is useful at home may not be permitted on a managed device.

Keep your browser and extensions under control

Browser updates can include security fixes. Enable automatic updates where appropriate and restart the browser when it asks you to complete an update. Review extensions because they may be able to read or change information on websites you visit. Remove extensions you no longer need and check the publisher and requested permissions before installing a new one.

Pay attention to browser warnings

Safe Browsing and similar browser protections can warn about known phishing pages, malware downloads or other unsafe behavior. Treat a warning as a reason to stop and verify the site, not as an inconvenience to bypass. Security services can make mistakes, and new threats may not yet be known, so warnings are one layer of protection rather than a guarantee.

Use a simple link-checking routine

  1. Pause when a message creates urgency or asks for account details.
  2. Inspect the complete domain, not only the visible display text.
  3. Open the official service through a known bookmark or typed address.
  4. Check whether the request makes sense for the account or task.
  5. Do not download unexpected files or enable risky settings to view them.
  6. Report suspicious messages through the service or organization's approved process.

Worked example: a fake software update

A pop-up says your browser is out of date and offers a download button. Instead of clicking it, open the browser's own settings menu and check for updates there. If the browser says it is current, close the pop-up. This reduces the chance of installing a fake updater from an advertisement or compromised page.

Practice task

  1. Find the official website address for a service you use regularly.
  2. Explain why HTTPS alone does not prove that a site is genuine.
  3. Review the browser extensions installed on a device you control.
  4. Describe what you should do when a browser warns that a download may be unsafe.

Quick review

  • Check the complete domain before sharing sensitive details.
  • HTTPS encrypts the connection but does not certify the website's honesty.
  • Download software from trusted sources and keep it updated.
  • Browser warnings should be investigated rather than ignored.
