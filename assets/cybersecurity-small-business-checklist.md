# Small Business Cybersecurity Checklist

A practical, printable checklist for small teams. Use it as a starting point, then adapt it to your systems, legal obligations, contracts, and risk profile.

## 1. Accounts and identity
- [ ] Every employee has an individual account; shared accounts are avoided where practical.
- [ ] Multi-factor authentication is enabled for email, administration, finance, cloud, and other high-impact accounts.
- [ ] Former staff and unused accounts are disabled promptly.
- [ ] Administrator access is limited to people who need it.
- [ ] Recovery methods are documented and protected.

## 2. Devices and software
- [ ] Operating systems and important applications receive security updates.
- [ ] Supported endpoint protection is enabled where appropriate.
- [ ] Screen locks and device passwords are required.
- [ ] Lost-device procedures are documented.
- [ ] Unsupported or unnecessary software is removed.

## 3. Data
- [ ] The business knows where important data is stored.
- [ ] Sensitive data is shared only with people who need it.
- [ ] Backups cover important business information.
- [ ] Backup access is protected separately from ordinary user access.
- [ ] Data retention and deletion practices are documented.

## 4. Email and phishing
- [ ] Staff know how to report suspicious messages.
- [ ] Payment or account-change requests are verified through a trusted channel.
- [ ] High-risk accounts use stronger authentication.
- [ ] Staff receive practical security awareness guidance.

## 5. Recovery
- [ ] Critical systems and contacts are listed.
- [ ] The person responsible for incident coordination is known.
- [ ] Backups are periodically tested for restoration.
- [ ] Recovery priorities are documented.
- [ ] The plan is reviewed after tests or incidents.

## 6. Suppliers and cloud services
- [ ] Important vendors are inventoried.
- [ ] Security and privacy requirements are reviewed before adopting important services.
- [ ] Access granted to vendors is limited and reviewed.
- [ ] Contracts and service documentation are stored where the team can find them.

## 7. Incident response
- [ ] Staff know where to report a suspected incident.
- [ ] Evidence is preserved rather than casually deleted.
- [ ] Important accounts can be secured quickly.
- [ ] Relevant legal, contractual, insurance, or regulatory contacts are identified where applicable.

### Use this checklist carefully
This is general educational guidance, not a legal, compliance, or incident-response determination. For a current security baseline, compare your controls with authoritative guidance such as NIST and CISA.
