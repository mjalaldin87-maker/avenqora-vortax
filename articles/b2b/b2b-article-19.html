B2B Sales-to-Delivery Handoff Checklist: From Closed-Won to Customer Onboarding

A B2B sales-to-delivery handoff transfers the customer's goals, agreed commitments and open questions from the sales team to the people responsible for implementation and service. A structured handoff reduces repeated discovery, unclear ownership and avoidable surprises during the first stage of customer onboarding.

A B2B sales-to-delivery handoff checklist should preserve the context that helped the customer choose the service. When a deal reaches closed-won, delivery teams need more than a signed order form. They need to know what problem the customer expects the project to solve, what was agreed, who is involved and what must happen next.

Define the handoff trigger and owner

Choose a clear event that starts the process, such as a contract becoming effective, an order being accepted or a deal reaching an approved closed-won stage. Avoid relying on a vague expectation that the salesperson will remember to send a message. Assign one sales owner to complete the record and one delivery owner to accept it.

For larger accounts, a short handoff meeting may be useful. For routine deals, a required CRM form and a scheduled internal review can be enough. The process should be proportionate to deal size, technical complexity and customer risk.

Customer goals and measures of success

Write the customer's desired outcomes in plain language. Avoid vague phrases such as “improve efficiency” without explaining what process should improve or how the customer will judge progress. Record a baseline when one is available and agree who will confirm the result during onboarding.

Distinguish the outcome from the deliverable. A new dashboard may be a deliverable; faster reporting may be the desired outcome. This distinction helps delivery teams explain which measures they can influence and which depend on customer behaviour, data quality or external systems.

Commercial terms and scope boundaries

Link to the approved contract, order form and statement of work rather than retyping a summary that could become inaccurate. Highlight the included products, services, quantities, dates, renewal terms, exclusions and change process. Flag any non-standard discount, service promise or delivery date that needs an owner.

Sales notes are not a substitute for the signed agreement. If a customer mentions a verbal promise that is not documented, mark it as an open question and escalate it before delivery commits. Do not silently convert an assumption into a contractual obligation.

Stakeholders and communication plan

List the people who will approve decisions, provide information, use the solution and resolve technical questions. Identify the preferred communication channel and meeting cadence. Confirm whether the customer expects a single point of contact or separate commercial, project and technical leads.

Also record accessibility, language, time-zone or procurement constraints that affect project coordination. Share personal contact details only with people who need them for the work and follow the organisation's privacy and data-handling rules.

Technical readiness and customer dependencies

Technical requirements can include identity setup, data formats, API access, network allowlists, security review, migration windows and vendor approvals. Record dependencies with an owner and a due date. If a requirement is unknown, state that it must be discovered rather than marking it as completed.

  • Which systems must connect, and who owns each system?
  • Does the customer need a security or privacy review?
  • Who will supply test data, credentials or sample files through an approved secure channel?
  • Are there blackout periods, change freezes or business deadlines?
  • What must be ready before kickoff or configuration can begin?

Never put passwords, private keys or API tokens in a CRM note or handoff email. Use the approved secure method for exchanging access material and limit it to named recipients.

Readiness review before the kickoff

Before inviting the customer to a kickoff, the delivery owner should check the record for completeness. Confirm the signed scope, customer sponsor, intended outcomes, technical dependencies and first set of actions. If key information is missing, assign an owner and decide whether kickoff can proceed with a clear discovery agenda.

  1. Confirm commercial approval and effective dates.
  2. Check that scope and exclusions match the signed documents.
  3. Verify named stakeholders and the decision-making route.
  4. Review technical prerequisites and unresolved risks.
  5. Agree the first milestone and customer communication owner.
  6. Send the customer a concise agenda and list of preparation tasks.

Measure handoff quality

Track process measures that help teams improve the handoff: time from closed-won to accepted handoff, percentage of records completed before kickoff, number of missing critical fields, repeated discovery questions and early delivery escalations. Use these measures to find a process issue, not to punish people for complex deals.

Review a sample of handoffs with sales and delivery together. Look for patterns such as missing customer goals, undocumented technical assumptions or unclear decision ownership. Update the required fields and training when the same gap appears repeatedly.

Frequently asked questions

When should a B2B sales handoff happen?

Set the handoff trigger around an approved commercial milestone, such as a signed agreement or accepted order. Start the internal transfer early enough for delivery to review scope and dependencies before customer kickoff. The exact trigger should match the company's contract and approval process.

Who owns the customer after the deal closes?

Ownership depends on the operating model, but it should never be ambiguous. Name the commercial contact, implementation lead and ongoing customer-success owner where those roles are separate. Tell the customer who to contact for project decisions, support requests and future commercial discussions.

Should sales promises be included in the handoff?

Yes, but distinguish documented contractual commitments from informal expectations or unresolved statements. Link to the signed agreement and record any promise that needs confirmation. Delivery should not assume that a sales note changes the contract or authorises work outside the agreed scope.

What if important information is missing?

Mark the missing item as an open question, assign a named owner and agree a date for resolution. Decide whether the gap blocks kickoff or can be handled as a discovery action. Do not hide uncertainty by entering guessed values into the customer record.

How can teams improve handoff quality?

Use a short mandatory checklist, define an acceptance owner and review early delivery problems with sales. Track missing fields and repeated clarification requests, then improve the form or training where patterns appear. Keep the process proportionate so routine deals are not slowed by unnecessary administration.

Key takeaway

A strong B2B handoff gives delivery teams the customer context, contractual facts, technical prerequisites, stakeholders and next steps they need to start well. Make the handoff a shared responsibility, link to source documents, flag assumptions and confirm readiness before the first customer meeting.
