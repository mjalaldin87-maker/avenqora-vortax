B2B Invoice Payment Terms: Due Dates, Deposits and Late Payments

A payment term is useful only when both businesses understand when an invoice is due, what must be supplied for approval and what happens if payment is delayed. This guide to B2B invoice payment terms covers practical options, contract wording, invoice controls and a respectful follow-up process.

Prepared by Avenqora Vortax Editorial DeskNovember 19, 202612 min read

B2B invoice payment terms explain when a business customer is expected to pay for goods or services. Clear terms reduce avoidable disputes and help suppliers forecast cash flow. They should be agreed before work begins, reflected in the contract or order and repeated accurately on the invoice.

What are B2B invoice payment terms?

B2B invoice payment terms are the agreed rules that state when and how one business pays another. They may specify a number of days, an advance deposit, milestone payments, an early-payment discount, accepted methods and a process for resolving invoice disputes.

Terms and due dates are related but not identical. “Net 30” describes a payment period; a specific calendar due date removes ambiguity about when the customer should settle the invoice. Confirm which event starts the clock, such as invoice receipt, acceptance, delivery or milestone approval.

What do Net 15, Net 30 and due on receipt mean?

Net 15 generally means the undisputed invoice balance is due within 15 days of the agreed starting event; Net 30 means 30 days. “Due on receipt” asks for payment when the invoice is received, but the contract should still explain the exact process and exceptions.

Do not assume the same term has the same legal effect in every country. UK government guidance explains specific default and agreed payment rules for UK commercial transactions. Other jurisdictions may set different requirements, so check the rules applying to the contract and customer location.

How should a business choose the right payment terms?

Choose terms by balancing delivery cost, buyer approval cycles, relationship history and the supplier's ability to finance work while waiting. A new project with significant up-front materials may need a deposit, while a repeat low-risk service may support a simpler invoice after delivery.

  • Match the cash commitment: if the supplier must pay for materials or contractors early, consider a deposit or staged billing.
  • Check buyer process: ask who approves invoices, whether a purchase order is required and which portal or format the buyer uses.
  • Define acceptance: state how the customer confirms delivery or raises a documented dispute.
  • Protect both sides: explain cancellation, partial delivery, refunds, late charges and dispute handling in the signed agreement.
  • Review concentration: long terms for one large customer can create cash-flow risk even when that customer is reliable.

What should a B2B invoice include?

A useful invoice identifies the seller, buyer, transaction, amount, currency, issue date and due date, then gives the customer the information needed to approve and pay it. Legal invoice requirements vary, so treat this as an operational checklist and confirm tax and statutory fields locally.

How can suppliers reduce invoice delays?

Send an accurate invoice promptly, to the correct department and through the buyer's required channel. Ask the customer to confirm receipt, keep the purchase order and delivery evidence together, and track each invoice from submission through approval, dispute and payment.

  1. Confirm the billing contact, required portal and purchase-order reference before work starts.
  2. Agree whether the invoice is triggered by delivery, a time period or milestone acceptance.
  3. Submit the invoice promptly and retain proof of sending or portal receipt.
  4. Record the expected due date and the person responsible for follow-up.
  5. Reconcile bank receipts against invoice numbers and resolve short payments or deductions.

What is a practical late-payment follow-up process?

Start by checking the agreed terms, invoice delivery and any dispute before chasing payment. Send a professional reminder with the invoice number, amount, due date and payment instructions. If the issue remains unresolved, contact the buyer's accounts team and document the agreed next step.

In the UK, the Small Business Commissioner explains that statutory interest and fixed compensation may be available for qualifying late commercial payments, subject to applicable rules and any legitimate dispute. This is a UK example only; do not copy statutory rates or remedies into an international contract without checking local law.

How do invoice terms affect cash flow?

Longer payment terms delay the point when earned revenue becomes available cash. Forecast expected receipts by due date, include a realistic allowance for disputed or late invoices and compare the timing with payroll, supplier bills, tax and other operating commitments.

Illustrative example: A service provider completes a $6,000 project. The parties agree to a $2,000 start deposit, $2,400 after an approved delivery milestone and $1,600 after final acceptance. The supplier can forecast three receipts, while the customer sees what evidence triggers each payment. This is a negotiated example, not a standard legal template.

Common mistakes in B2B payment terms

  • Writing “Net 30” without agreeing when the 30 days start.
  • Using invoice terms that conflict with the signed contract or purchase order.
  • Sending invoices to the person who commissioned the work instead of the correct payment team.
  • Leaving out a required purchase-order reference or acceptance record.
  • Charging a late fee without confirming the contract and applicable law.
  • Failing to record partial payments, disputes and promised dates.

Frequently asked questions

What are standard B2B invoice payment terms?

Net 15 and Net 30 are widely used examples, but there is no single worldwide standard. The right term depends on the contract, buyer process, delivery cost, negotiating position and local law. State the exact due date and the event that starts the payment period.

Does Net 30 mean 30 business days?

Not automatically. Net 30 usually refers to a 30-day period, but the contract should define whether days are calendar or business days and what happens when a due date falls on a holiday. Never rely on an abbreviation to settle an ambiguity.

Should a small business request a deposit?

A deposit can be sensible when work requires materials, reserved capacity or substantial upfront labour. Agree the amount, what it covers, whether it is refundable and how later milestones are billed. The right structure depends on the project and applicable commercial rules.

When is a B2B invoice officially late?

That depends on the agreed contract and governing law. In the UK, government guidance provides specific rules for agreed dates and default periods; other countries differ. Check the correct jurisdiction and confirm that the invoice was received and accepted where the rules require it.

What should a supplier do when a customer disputes an invoice?

Ask for the disputed item and reason in writing, compare it with the contract and delivery evidence, and resolve the issue through the agreed process. Keep the undisputed amount, payment schedule and communication record clear, and seek professional advice before legal escalation.

Key takeaway

Clear B2B payment terms begin before the first invoice. Agree the trigger, due date, deposit or milestone structure, required purchase-order details and dispute route in writing. Then send accurate invoices, track receipt and follow up consistently while respecting the law applying to the transaction.
